# Expert-validation protocol (Phase 17)

The study that turns "our explanations are interpretable" from an assertion into
a measurement. `CLAUDE.md` §1 names expert-validated interpretability as
contribution #2 and the paper's headline differentiator, because the Phase 3
review (`docs/related_work.md`) found sports-XAI explanations are almost never
validated with practitioners.

---

## 1. The research question

> Do the model's span-level explanations identify text that a sports-familiar
> reader accepts as a plausible cue for the named construct, more often than
> length-matched random spans of the same text?

The comparison clause is the whole question. An approval rate on its own is
uninterpretable - raters are agreeable, and nobody knows whether 78% is good.

## 2. What raters are asked, and what they are explicitly not asked

**Asked:** given a highlighted phrase, its sentence, and a construct name - is
the phrase a plausible textual cue for that construct? `yes` / `partly` / `no`.

**Not asked:** anything about a person's psychological state. Three reasons, and
all three must survive review:

1. **There is no person.** Every record is synthetic (`synth_precomp_v1`,
   OPEN-011). A question about an athlete's anxiety has no truth-maker.
2. **`docs/ethics.md` forbids it.** Inferring mental-health status of an
   identifiable person is out of scope for this project, and an instrument that
   asked raters to practise it on synthetic text would normalise exactly the
   misuse the paper warns against.
3. **Cue plausibility is the quantity the contribution claims.** The claim is
   "our explanations are sensible to informed readers", not "our model is
   clinically accurate" - which this project cannot claim and must not imply.

This framing is also what makes OPEN-004 survivable. Judging whether *"my hands
won't stop shaking"* is a plausible cue for somatic arousal needs sports
familiarity and the rubric in `docs/annotation_guidelines.md`, not a clinical
licence.

## 3. Instrument design

Generated by `src/explainability/study.py::build_sheet` via
`python scripts/run_explain.py`. Default sheet ≈ 90 items, ≈ 25 minutes.

| Item type | n (default) | Purpose | Rater sees the type? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `model` | 60 | The model's genuine top span for a construct it predicted | no |
| `random_span` | 15 | **The floor.** A uniformly-chosen span of the *same word length* from the *same record*, same construct | no |
| `mismatched` | 9 | **Attention check.** A genuine model span paired with a *different* construct | no |

Three design choices worth defending:

- **Length matching.** Without it the control differs from a model item in
  content *and* length, and lower approval could be explained by either.
- **Same record.** A control drawn from a different record would also differ in
  topic. Same record isolates *which span*, which is the only thing under test.
- **Three-point scale, not five.** On a five-point Likert, raters use the middle
  as "I'm not sure", which is a different quantity from "partly plausible" and
  cannot be separated afterwards. Three also keeps the kappa contingency table
  populated; a 5×5 kappa on ~90 items has single-digit cells and an unstable
  expected-agreement term.

**Blinding.** Item types live only in `rating_sheet_KEY.json`, written to a
separate file from `rating_sheet.md`. `RatingSheet.render()` cannot emit them
and a test asserts it. **Never send the KEY file to a rater.**

## 4. Raters - the honest position (OPEN-004)

At the time of writing **no coach or sport-psychology practitioner has been
recruited.** A2 is a sports-familiar teammate, not a practitioner.

The rater population is written onto the sheet's own cover page at generation
time, before any result is seen. That is deliberate: it fixes the claim at the
moment of data collection, so the wording cannot drift upward once the numbers
look good.

| Raters actually obtained | How the paper must describe it |
|---|---|
| ≥1 coach / sport-psych practitioner, ≥2 raters total | Expert validation. The full contribution #2 claim. |
| 2 sports-familiar students (current default) | **Pilot expert-review with sports-familiar student raters.** Report as preliminary; name the absence of practitioner raters as a limitation. |
| 1 rater only | Approval rates and control margin only. **No kappa** - inter-rater agreement needs two raters, and reporting a single rater's self-consistency as agreement would be misleading. |
| Owner only | Self-audit. Last resort. State it plainly; it is a materially weaker claim because the author rating their own system's explanations is not independent. |

Two raters minimum, working **independently and without discussion** before
submission. The kappa is meaningless if they confer.

**Recruitment, still to do.** Contact SRMIST sports department coaches and any
sport-psychology staff. The ask is 25 minutes and one Markdown file. Pair it
with the A3 consented-donation recruitment (`docs/recruitment.md`) - same
population, one conversation.

## 5. Procedure

1. `python scripts/run_explain.py` → generates `reports/explain/rating_sheet.md`
   and `rating_sheet_KEY.json`.
2. Send **only** `rating_sheet.md` to each rater, plus
   `docs/annotation_guidelines.md` for the full construct rubric.
3. Raters work independently. No discussion until both have returned.
4. Each returns a JSON file mapping `item_id → yes|partly|no`
   (e.g. `reports/explain/ratings_R1.json`).
5. `python scripts/run_explain.py --score-study reports/explain/ratings_R1.json reports/explain/ratings_R2.json`
6. Results land in `reports/explain/study_result.json` and are written into
   `reports/explain/explain.md`.

## 6. What is reported, and the thresholds fixed in advance

Fixed here, before data collection, so they cannot be chosen to fit the result.

| Quantity | Reported as | Pre-registered expectation |
|---|---|---|
| Approval rate, model items | Both lenient (`yes`+`partly`) **and** strict (`yes` only) | - |
| **Control margin** (model − random) | The headline number | **> 0**, or the explanations have demonstrated nothing |
| Mismatch approval | Attention check | **≤ 0.30**; above this, raters are not discriminating between constructs and the sheet is suspect |
| Cohen's κ (model items, 2 raters) | Unweighted, three categories | Reported whatever it is |
| Per-construct approval | Table | Reported whatever it is |

**Both approval thresholds are reported, always.** A single approval rate with
an unstated `partly` policy is the easiest number in this study to quietly
optimise.

**κ is unweighted** - the conservative choice. Weighted κ credits a
`yes`/`partly` disagreement partially and returns a higher number on an ordered
scale. Erring low and saying so is the defensible direction.

**κ can be undefined**, and `cohens_kappa` returns `None` rather than 0.0 when
it is. If both raters use a single category throughout, expected agreement is
1.0; reporting κ=0 ("no agreement beyond chance") about raters who agreed on
every item would be flatly wrong. Report the raw agreement rate with a note.

## 7. Interpreting the outcomes - including the ones we would rather not get

| Faithfulness | Expert plausibility | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| High | High | The explanations describe the model *and* make sense. The contribution stands. |
| **High** | **Low** | **The model learned the generator, not the construct.** The explanations honestly describe a system keying on template giveaways. This is a real finding and strong evidence for the OPEN-011 limitation - report it, do not bury it. |
| Low | High | The most dangerous cell. The explanations look sensible and do not describe the model. This is what a coach-facing dashboard would ship if only the expert study were run, and it is why `faithfulness.py` exists. |
| Low | Low | The attribution method is not working on this model. Report and investigate before claiming anything. |

The second row is a live possibility here, not a hypothetical. Planning for it
in advance is what stops it becoming a result nobody knows how to write up.

## 8. Ethics

- All text is synthetic. No consent is required from any text subject because
  there is none.
- Raters are adults giving ~25 minutes voluntarily. Tell them what the data is
  for, that ratings are reported only in aggregate, and that they may stop at
  any time.
- Do not record rater names in the repository. Use `R1`, `R2`.
- Covered by the SRMIST exemption determination in `docs/ethics.md` §3.4
  (OPEN-005 tracks getting that in writing).

## 9. Limitations to state in the paper

1. Student raters, not practitioners (OPEN-004), unless recruitment succeeds.
2. Synthetic text, so plausibility is judged on generated language rather than
   real athlete speech (OPEN-011).
3. Small item count; per-construct rates rest on ~6 items each and should carry
   wide intervals or be reported as indicative only.
4. Plausibility is not correctness. A rater accepting a cue does not make it a
   valid psychological indicator.
