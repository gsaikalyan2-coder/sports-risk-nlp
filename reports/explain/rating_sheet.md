# Explanation plausibility rating sheet

**Rater population declared for this sheet:** sports-familiar student raters (pilot expert-review; NOT coaches or sport-psychology practitioners -- see OPEN-004)

## What you are judging

Each item shows a **highlighted phrase**, the **sentence it came from**,
and one **construct name**. Answer one question:

> Is the highlighted phrase a plausible textual cue for that construct?

Answer `yes`, `partly`, or `no` in the Rating column.

- **yes** -- a reader who knows the construct definition would accept this
  phrase as evidence for it.
- **partly** -- the phrase is related but weak, ambiguous, or needs the rest
  of the sentence to work.
- **no** -- the phrase is not evidence for this construct.

### Three things to keep in mind

1. You are **not** judging whether the writer is anxious, stressed, or at
   risk. You are judging whether the phrase is a sensible cue for the named
   construct. There is no real person here -- all text is computer-generated.
2. Judge only the highlighted phrase. The sentence is context, not the item.
3. Some items will look odd. That is expected and it is part of the design.
   Rate what you see; do not try to work out what the 'right' answer is.

## Construct definitions

- **appraisal_orientation** -- Whether the athlete frames the upcoming competition as a CHALLENGE (an opportunity; resources judged sufficient for the demand) or a THREAT (a danger; resources judged insufficient).
- **attentional_focus** -- Direction and stability of attention — task-focused concentration on controllables versus distraction by irrelevant cues, the outcome, the crowd, rivals, or media.
- **burnout_signal** -- Emotional and physical exhaustion, reduced sense of accomplishment, or sport devaluation — the three ABQ dimensions.
- **cognitive_anxiety** -- Negative expectations and worry about performance — fear of failure, rumination, self-doubt, or catastrophising about the upcoming competition.
- **coping_style** -- How the athlete manages competitive pressure — TASK-FOCUSED coping (planning, reappraisal, routine, seeking support, problem-solving) versus AVOIDANCE coping (disengagement, distraction, denial, suppression, withdrawal).
- **motivation_orientation** -- Whether the expressed drive is APPROACH-focused (pursuing success, mastery, improvement) or AVOIDANCE-focused (avoiding failure, shame, criticism, loss).
- **perceived_stress** -- Sense that competitive or surrounding demands exceed available resources — feeling overloaded, pressured, stretched, or unable to control what is coming.
- **resilience** -- Expressed capacity to maintain or recover psychological functioning under competitive pressure — bounce-back after setbacks, steadiness, confidence in handling adversity.
- **self_confidence** -- Belief in one's own ability to perform well and meet the competitive demand; certainty about executing the plan under pressure.
- **somatic_anxiety** -- Perception of bodily arousal or physiological tension tied to the competition — racing heart, butterflies, shakiness, nausea, tight muscles, disturbed sleep.
